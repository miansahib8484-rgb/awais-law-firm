Adverse Possession in Pakistan: Can Long Occupation Override a Registered Title?

By Muhammad Awais Ahsan Joiya · Published May 4, 2026 · Awais Law Associates

Adverse possession — continuous, open, and hostile occupation of property for a statutory period — is one of the most litigated doctrines in Pakistani property law, and one of the most frequently misunderstood by property owners and occupants alike. Property owners in Lahore, including those in DHA and other high-value residential areas, regularly consult a civil litigation law firm in Lahore or a property lawyer in DHA Lahore as soon as unauthorised occupation is discovered.

The 12-Year Rule

Under the Limitation Act 1908, a person in adverse possession for 12 continuous years may, in certain circumstances, extinguish the original owner's right to sue for possession. This is an important distinction to hold onto: extinguishing the right to sue for possession is not automatically the same as the occupant acquiring formal, registered title — the two outcomes can diverge significantly in practice, and each depends on the specific facts and the relief actually sought in court.

Recent Lahore High Court Approach

Lahore High Court decisions have held that adverse possession claims cannot succeed where the occupant has acknowledged the true owner's title — even informally, such as through a rent payment, a request for permission, or correspondence recognising the owner's rights. This means the occupant's own conduct during the occupation period is often the deciding factor, not simply the length of time in possession.

Registered Title vs Possession

Courts consistently hold that a registered title carries a strong presumption of ownership. An adverse possession claimant carries the burden of proving occupation was hostile, continuous, exclusive, open, and notorious — all five elements simultaneously and for the full statutory period. Falling short on any single element is typically fatal to the claim.

Conclusion

For both registered owners and long-term occupants, the practical lesson is the same: document everything. Owners should act promptly on any unauthorised occupation, and occupants relying on possession should understand how easily an informal acknowledgment of the true owner's rights can undermine an otherwise long-standing claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does paying property tax on a piece of land establish adverse possession?

Not by itself. Tax payment can be one supporting factor among several, but courts look at the full picture — exclusivity, hostility, and continuity of possession — rather than any single act in isolation.

What should a registered owner do if they discover unauthorised occupation of their property?

Act promptly. Delay can complicate a later claim, since the occupant's period of continuous possession keeps running until it is legally interrupted through a court filing or equivalent formal action.
